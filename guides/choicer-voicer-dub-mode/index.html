CHOICER VOICER GUIDE

The Choicer Voicer Dub Mode guide

Choose a scene, assign performers, rehearse timing, record a complete pass, and review the strongest take.

Last verified: August 27, 2026
Open Dub StudioRead the steps ↓
01

What Dub Mode changes

Dub Mode shifts the session from short voice choices toward a sustained performance against a scene. Players listen for entrances, pauses, emotional changes, and exchanges between characters. The goal is not a perfect studio replacement; it is a coherent take the room can watch and replay.

02

Prepare a permitted scene

Use only media you created, licensed, or received permission to transform. Check its duration, number of characters, speaking pace, and whether the performers can hear the source without feeding playback into the microphone. Headphones make timing easier and reduce echo.

03

Assign characters and timing jobs

For one player, mark where each character begins and change delivery clearly. For a group, assign characters before recording and nominate one person to call the start. If a scene is fast, practice entrances rather than memorizing every word. A short timing note is more useful than a long rule explanation.

04

Rehearse once, then record a full pass

Watch the scene once, rehearse once, and record a complete attempt. Stopping after every imperfect line usually removes the chemistry that makes a dub entertaining. Keep loudness comfortable and use distance from the microphone to control stronger characters.

05

Review with a purpose

Compare timing, clarity, energy, and chemistry. Decide whether the next take will change pace, character contrast, or line entrances—not all of them at once. Import a recording into the local Dub Studio to preview it, mark a strong section, balance playback, and prepare captions.

06

Use Packs to change the session

A community Pack can provide a ready-made scene and creator credit, while the custom Pack workbench can select spoken moments from permitted local media. Open the Pack detail first to verify source information and known metadata; do not assume that a downloadable archive grants redistribution rights.

07

Export and share responsibly

The Clip Maker helps preview vertical, square, or landscape framing and caption ideas. It does not claim final MP4 rendering until a media pipeline is connected. Ask every recorded performer before publishing a voice clip, and do not include private notifications, names, or conversations in shared captures.

08

Plan entrances instead of memorizing everything

Mark the first word or action that tells each performer to begin. For a fast exchange, write a short cue sheet with character, start time, and emotional direction rather than copying a complete copyrighted script. Rehearse the handoff between performers and keep the source audio in headphones. A cue should help someone enter on time without covering the screen or forcing them to read a paragraph while acting. If the supplied Pack page includes a static speaker-and-time guide, use it to divide roles before loading the archive.

09

Choose the take the group can actually finish

A complete, understandable take is usually more entertaining than a technically perfect opening followed by repeated stops. Decide in advance whether the session allows one restart, unlimited rehearsal, or a single live attempt. After playback, identify the strongest moment and one fix for the next pass. Import a recording into Dub Studio only when everyone captured has agreed. The current tools can organize and preview local media, but they do not grant permission to republish a source scene, character, soundtrack, or another person's voice.
